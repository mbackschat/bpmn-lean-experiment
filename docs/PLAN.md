# Plan

Delivery order, budgets and the resume point. The [rewrite-2 proposal](REWRITE-2-PROPOSAL.md) owns the disclosure, the design (KD, AD, HD and PD items), the promise register and the calibration this plan budgets from; the [kernel architecture](kernel/ARCHITECTURE-SPEC.md) owns the guardrails and the definition of done; the [coverage target](PRACTICAL-CIB7-COVERAGE-TARGET.md#delivery-task-list) owns each row's acceptance; the [kernel status map](kernel/KERNEL-STATUS-MAP.md) owns what is implemented and open per row. This file and the status map change only at a milestone or track closure, or at a stop.

## Goal

The goal is Product 2: the BPM platform, deployed and used by a third party on their own BPMN models, on the engine (owner, 2026-11-17). [Practical CIB7 Coverage](PRACTICAL-CIB7-COVERAGE-TARGET.md), accepted on 2026-11-14, was a milestone toward it. Scope is settled in the documents the [owner intents](OWNER-INTENTS-REGISTER.md) list; this plan selects what is built and when, never what is in scope. Published tags stay as they are, `v0.2.0-rc.1` at `16c5b9f6`; no new version, tag or tag move is selected.

**Goal acceptance, proposed for the owner.** The plan reaches the goal when a third party can deploy each of the census models (the 54 public Camunda examples weigh most; the 55 CIB Seven models are mostly webapp and test resources) and either run it or receive a refusal that names the element, a stable code and its classification: outside the CIB Seven `2.2.0` ceiling, excluded by the settled documents, or awaiting a named owner decision. No in-ceiling, in-scope barrier stays unimplemented; the census runs under both profiles; and the platform gates (PD4, the boundary guards, the showcase milestones) are green. A count such as T1a's 69 of 109 is a progress measure, never acceptance.

## Order gates

- The core shape (K0) passes the KD7 guards before any feature is moved onto it, and every open row maps onto it. K0 holds every IL, input, state and hook shape change the later stages need (AD3's fields, HD4's `cancelInstance`, AD8's job shapes: the `job` open kind with its detail, `Hooks.failJob`, the running-transition case, the job incident subject and the wider trigger), each refused with a stable code until its stage fills it, so no stage changes a shape under the proofs K0 ports and K5 builds. A4's compensation-context state is not yet designed; it comes after K5, and its re-proof is priced when it is designed.
- Parity (K2) equals the current kernel on every retained scenario, population scenario, committed publication and admission decision, except the designated changes below, each with its own red/green witness. A mismatch is classified (kernel defect, current-kernel defect, designated change), never absorbed by adjusting the baseline. The held-state interpretation review (PD2) is approved before K2 starts, because the held state is one of its designated changes.
- **Every stage lands green.** A stage ports every law and proof its own change breaks, because the full Lean library gates `verify-complete` and no work-in-progress commit lands. K0 therefore carries the port of the closed lanes (formerly K4), and K3 ports the `Laws.Step` lemmas its closure change breaks. K5 starts with its costliest obligations (the E-units and settle-agreement), so its calibration is re-checked first.
- A track's fill starts only when its row fixtures, scenarios and CIB evidence exist, so rows turn red to green against an answer fixed before the code: an A stage takes its new rows' CIB answers from local single-scenario probes, which CLAUDE.md allows one at a time under a timeout, so one hosted run lands it; a stage that needs an oracle suite run takes a second hosted run, recorded in its row. Agreement between Lean and TypeScript is a transcription check, never evidence of meaning.
- A stage an owner item blocks does not start; independent stages continue. The blocked part of a stage is named in its row and in [owner-blocked work](#owner-blocked-work).
- **Stop conditions.** Each stage counts progress in the units its budget multiplies (its row's "Progress units"). A stage that has used a quarter of its budget with less than a sixth of its units done (1.5× the projected rate) stops and is reported to the owner with the boundary reached and the next decision. A stage that misses its budget stops, is recorded for the owner, and work continues on the next independent stage. A budget is never renewed silently.

## Budgets

**Unit: the active hour**, a clock hour in which the project delivers, with up to three parallel agents and the root. The first rewrite (`ffa9ed391..b176b25fe`, 2026-11-08 to 11-14) took 119 active hours over 7 calendar days, about 17 active hours per day; every proportion below is stated against those 119. The proposal's earlier agent-day figures are the audits' estimates from before calibration; where this plan uses one, an agent-day is 8 hours, taken as 8 active hours without a delivery factor, which may understate it.

- **Delivery factor (measured 2026-11-17 from git).** Over that range, the paths `Kernel`, `packages/kernel/src`, `packages/temporal-host/src`, `packages/engine-api/src`, `platform` and `packages/bpmn-source/src` gained 88,620 lines (the insertions of the end-to-end diff, `git diff --shortstat`, which also deleted 30,910) in 821 commits; summed over the commits they gross 126,018, the difference being rework, which is part of the delivery cost, and all paths together gross about 1.49 million with generated evidence, fixtures and lockfiles, which are excluded. That is about 745 delivered lines per active hour, reviews, hosted cycles and fix rounds included; the gross count would give about 1,060 and smaller factors, so the end-to-end count is the conservative choice. Spike rates were about 27–30 proved Lean lines per minute and about 100 TypeScript lines per minute, so raw spike time converts to active hours at about **×2.5 for Lean** and **×8 for TypeScript**. Where a spike did not split its time, the budget shows both ends. Limits: gross insertions; a port of known designs; any hour with a commit counts. The proof wave inserted about 52,000 `Laws` and `Proofs` lines in 39 active hours, about 22 lines per minute, so ×2.5 is conservative for proof lines; the risk lies in composition, not in the rate.
- **Model factor.** A rate measured on T4a's prototype model is multiplied by ×3.0 first: T4c's ratio between T4a's prototype and a richer `ExtTreeMap` model of the join. Neither is `Kernel.State`; the step from model to the real kernel is **unmeasured** for those items (the E-units and the ghost core).
- **Measured** budgets come from a spike's rate times a counted unit. **Provisional** budgets come from the best estimate on file for an item no spike measured, with the reason; they are listed apart and never added to the measured total.

**Reading.** On measured cost the rewrite is about **3–6× the targeted fix**: about 51–87 active hours against 14–18, a difference of about 33–73 hours, a third to three fifths of the first rewrite. The recommendation does not rest on cost. It rests on what each side leaves unmeasured: the targeted fix's remainder is a lockstep simulation across schedules that must carry provenance through every generation of follow-on signals, now witnessed as necessary, while the rewrite's remainder stays inside the core it builds. This plan named its reversal criterion before measuring it (a costlier firing case under 20 minutes with no provenance); spike T4i did not meet it: its no-provenance case took 21.8 working minutes, about 16 once the replaced interface is subtracted, but the criterion fails on the firing kinds that leave follow-on signals. **Recommendation: the rewrite of the core (KD1, KD3).** Rejected alternatives: a hybrid keeping the counter but sorting by a schedule-independent key, which needs that key structural down to the scope chain, i.e. KD1; and KD1 over lists instead of maps, which keeps the keys but loses the exact state equality the diamond needs (T4a F7), so it would pay B's `Reorder` congruence instead of the container change.
