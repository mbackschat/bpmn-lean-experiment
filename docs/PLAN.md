# Plan

Delivery order, budgets and the resume point. The [rewrite-2 proposal](REWRITE-2-PROPOSAL.md) owns the disclosure, the design (KD, AD, HD and PD items), the promise register and the calibration this plan budgets from; the [kernel architecture](kernel/ARCHITECTURE-SPEC.md) owns the guardrails and the definition of done; the [coverage target](PRACTICAL-CIB7-COVERAGE-TARGET.md#delivery-task-list) owns each row's acceptance; the [kernel status map](kernel/KERNEL-STATUS-MAP.md) owns what is implemented and open per row. This file and the status map change only at a milestone or track closure, or at a stop.

## Goal

The goal is Product 2: the BPM platform, deployed and used by a third party on their own BPMN models, on the engine (owner, 2026-11-17). [Practical CIB7 Coverage](PRACTICAL-CIB7-COVERAGE-TARGET.md), accepted on 2026-11-14, was a milestone toward it. Scope is settled in the documents the [owner intents](OWNER-INTENTS-REGISTER.md) list; this plan selects what is built and when, never what is in scope. Published tags stay as they are, `v0.2.0-rc.1` at `16c5b9f6`; no new version, tag or tag move is selected.

**Goal acceptance (owner-approved, 2026-11-17).** The plan reaches the goal when a third party can deploy each of the census models (the 54 public Camunda examples weigh most; the 55 CIB Seven models are mostly webapp and test resources) and either run it or receive a refusal that names the element, a stable code and its classification: outside the CIB Seven `2.2.0` ceiling, excluded by the settled documents, or awaiting a named owner decision. No in-ceiling, in-scope barrier stays unimplemented; the census runs under both profiles; and the platform gates (PD4, the boundary guards, the showcase milestones) are green. A count such as T1a's 69 of 109 is a progress measure, never acceptance. The owner may add their own models at any time; the plan does not wait for them, and the root asks for them when the A stages start.

## Order gates

- The core shape (K0) passes the KD7 guards before any feature is moved onto it, and every open row maps onto it. K0 holds every IL, input, state and hook shape change the later stages need (AD3's fields, HD4's `cancelInstance`, HD7's `Bounds` record, AD8's job shapes: the `job` open kind with its detail, `Hooks.failJob`, the running-transition case, the job incident subject and the wider trigger) and, in K0.8's shape commit, the shapes the later stages were found to need (2026-11-19): the `ProfileOptions` fields of AD7's deployment-error options, A4's `escalation-rethrow-reenters-handler` and J's default retry cycle; AD1's IL list of retained extension attributes; the timer incident subject; the `Value` arms Double and Date with DMN `TypeRef` double (A3 keeps their fill); PD7's kernel stale-arrival flag; OD14's identity-bytes bound in `Bounds` (the kernel builds a called instance's identifier from its caller's, `Kernel/State.lean:451`); each refused with a stable code until its stage fills it, so no stage changes a shape under the proofs K0 ports and K5 builds. A4 adds no state: it lifts its four compensation-context codes by BPMN meaning (DC9).
- **K5 is split around the hook changes** (2026-11-19). Under KD2′ each footprint lemma unfolds its hook's body, so a later change to a hook body breaks it (T4c: 2 of 3 sampled hook changes and one behavior-neutral refactor). K5a (after K3) proves what does not unfold feature hook bodies; K5b (footprints, U1, the E-units and the settle-agreement assembly) starts only after the kernel parts of J, A2's runtime rows and A4's lifts have landed, with its unit count re-taken then, and K5b's `diamond_fail` and settle-agreement only after J covers every admitted asynchronous position (DC8). A stage that changes a kernel hook after K5b carries a priced upkeep line at T4c's rates: 0.4–3.7 min per touched hook, 2.2 min per U1 site and 12.2 min per touched E-unit, raw, at ×2.5. The rule covers every executable body a landed `Laws` or `Proofs` stage unfolds, not only hooks: `Step`, `Admission` and `Population` bodies included. So K5a starts after A1 and A2, whose admission changes (AD7's rules, AD2's registry) its first commit's `Laws.Admission.<F>` rows unfold; K6 waits for H2's compact registry, which its `Population.Timers` laws unfold; and P2's stale-arrival flag and A3's arms (if A3 lands after K5b) carry priced upkeep lines. The items of the [remaining-to-goal ledger] that change kernel executable code(REWRITE-2-PROPOSAL.md#remaining-to-goal-ledger-not-costly) land before K5b.
- Parity (K2) equals the current kernel on every retained scenario, population scenario, committed publication and admission decision, except the designated changes below, each with its own red/green witness. A mismatch is classified (kernel defect, current-kernel defect, designated change), never absorbed by adjusting the baseline. The held-state interpretation review (PD2) is approved before K0.8's held-state commit, because the held state is one of K2's designated changes and its kernel half lands in K0.8.
- **Every stage lands green.** A stage ports every law and proof its own change breaks, because the full Lean library gates `verify-complete` and no work-in-progress commit lands. K0 therefore carries the port of the closed lanes (formerly K4), and K3 ports the `Laws.Step` lemmas its closure change breaks. K5a starts, after A1 and A2, with KD4's admission rows and the L1 core, K5b with the E-units and the settle-agreement assembly, its costliest obligations, so each part's calibration is re-checked first.
- A track's fill starts only when its row fixtures, scenarios and CIB evidence exist, so rows turn red to green against an answer fixed before the code: a stage takes its new rows' CIB answers from local single-scenario probes, which CLAUDE.md allows one at a time under a timeout, through the probe procedure K0.8 builds first; a register entry lands in the same commit as the behavior it governs, and the stage's hosted CIB run confirms each answer before the stage lands on `main`, so one hosted run lands it; a stage that needs an oracle suite run takes a second hosted run, recorded in its row. Agreement between Lean and TypeScript is a transcription check, never evidence of meaning.
- A stage an owner item blocks does not start; independent stages continue. The blocked part of a stage is named in its row and in [owner-blocked work](#owner-blocked-work).
- **Stop conditions.** Each stage counts progress in the units its budget multiplies (its row's "Progress units"). A stage that has used a quarter of its budget with less than a sixth of its units done (1.5× the projected rate) stops and is reported to the owner with the boundary reached and the next decision. A stage that misses its budget stops, is recorded for the owner, and work continues on the next independent stage. A budget is never renewed silently.

## Budgets

**Unit: the active hour**, a clock hour in which the project delivers, with up to three parallel agents and the root. The first rewrite (`ffa9ed391..b176b25fe`, 2026-11-08 to 11-14) took 119 active hours over 7 calendar days, about 17 active hours per day; every proportion below is stated against those 119. The proposal's earlier agent-day figures are the audits' estimates from before calibration; where this plan uses one, an agent-day is 8 hours, taken as 8 active hours without a delivery factor, which may understate it.

- **Delivery factor (measured 2026-11-17 from git).** Over that range, the paths `Kernel`, `packages/kernel/src`, `packages/temporal-host/src`, `packages/engine-api/src`, `platform` and `packages/bpmn-source/src` gained 88,620 lines (the insertions of the end-to-end diff, `git diff --shortstat`, which also deleted 30,910) in 821 commits; summed over the commits they gross 126,018, the difference being rework, which is part of the delivery cost, and all paths together gross about 1.49 million with generated evidence, fixtures and lockfiles, which are excluded. That is about 745 delivered lines per active hour, reviews, hosted cycles and fix rounds included; the gross count would give about 1,060 and smaller factors, so the end-to-end count is the conservative choice. Spike rates were about 27–30 proved Lean lines per minute and about 100 TypeScript lines per minute, so raw spike time converts to active hours at about **×2.5 for Lean** and **×8 for TypeScript**. Where a spike did not split its time, the budget shows both ends. Limits: gross insertions; a port of known designs; any hour with a commit counts. The proof wave inserted about 52,000 `Laws` and `Proofs` lines in 39 active hours, about 22 lines per minute, so ×2.5 is conservative for proof lines; the risk lies in composition, not in the rate.
- **Model factor.** A rate measured on T4a's prototype model is multiplied by ×3.0 first: T4c's ratio between T4a's prototype and a richer `ExtTreeMap` model of the join. Neither is `Kernel.State`. On the real kernel since then: the ghost core over `Kernel.Step` with `Registry.hooks` (T4e), the Inclusive join's firing (T59 `fire_commutes`) and the Parallel join pair (T62, 0.3 h); the step from model to the real kernel stays **unmeasured** for the other E-units.
- **Measured** budgets come from a spike's rate times a counted unit. **Provisional** budgets come from the best estimate on file for an item no spike measured, with the reason; they are listed apart and never added to the measured total.

## Mechanism tracks, then rows

Rewrite-2 is delivered as stages, each a mechanism track in the sense of [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md#delivery): one combined review of meaning and structure per stage, which also approves the rows it closes. K stages are sequential, except that K5a waits for A1 and A2, K6 for H2, and K5b for the kernel parts of J, A2 and A4 (the upkeep rule above); A, H, J and P start when their "Depends on" column allows (A1, A2, A3, H1, H2, H3 after K2; A4 and J after K3; P after its H stages and K5b, so the kernel-touching breadth lands before the platform) and run beside K3–K6, up to three tracks at a time. Their files are disjoint except where K5b writes footprint lemmas into each `Laws.<F>` a feature stage also edits, which the K5 split orders. Profile bytes change through one root-owned profile commit per integration window, covering every running A, J and H stage, so retained evidence (1,583 files bind the `cibseven-2.2.0` digest) is re-retained once per window. An integration window is the span between two root integrations of the running tracks onto their branches; the window's profile commit carries the profile bytes, the re-retained evidence and every register entry whose profile behavior changes with them, so an entry still lands in the same commit as the behavior it governs. K0's increments are the shape stage's root-accepted `kernel-shape` commits (ARCH-SPEC:118 asks for shape edits batched at a track's start: K0 is that track).

2. **Pre-check (agent, before hand-back):** every edited Lean module elaborated, `Kernel.Run` on the increment's scenarios, the touched packages' type checks and unit tests, and the fast guards.
3. **Batch gate (root):** the full local gate (full Lean library, `Kernel.Run`, the dispatch and effects checks, every TypeScript type check, the unit suites, the fast guards and `git diff --check`; 232–442 s measured) runs once per batch of two or three pre-checked increments, not per increment; a failing batch is bisected over its increment commits, and the batch joins the track branch only when green.
