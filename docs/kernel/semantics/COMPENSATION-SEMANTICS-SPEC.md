# Compensation semantics

## Status

Adopted kernel owner document (W0 extraction, 2026-11-09). The kernel modules it cites are the executable authority; where they differ, the difference is a defect of one of them.

`Kernel.Feature.Compensation` ([Lean](../../../Kernel/Feature/Compensation.lean)) owns `FeatureSlots.compensation`: the records of completed compensable work (`Compensable`) and the compensation runs in progress (`CompensationRun`), together with Transaction cancellation's compensation step. It dispatches on no guarded type of its own; [propagation](PROPAGATION-SEMANTICS-SPEC.md) hands it a Compensation throw or a caught Cancel through `Signal.compensate`, and the pipeline reports completions through `Hooks.completed` and `Hooks.handlerCompleted`. Admission is in `Kernel.Admission.Work`, `Kernel.Admission.Propagation`, `Kernel.Admission.Scopes` and `Kernel.Admission.Profile`.

## Interpretation

### Eligibility and retention

- Only successfully completed work with a compensation handler is compensable: an Activity with a boundary Compensation Event and its associated handler, or a Sub-Process containing a Compensation Event Sub-Process (BPMN 2.0.2 Tables 10.88 and 10.89, §13.5.5). An omitted `activityRef` selects every visible eligible record; it never makes handler-free work eligible ([CBR account][cbr-normative], [experiment result][cre-result]). The IL lists a subject's handlers (`Envelope.compensationHandlers`), of which admission admits one (`compensation.handlerCount`), and marks a handler `Envelope.forCompensation`, which no Scope's implicit start begins.
- Eligibility is decided by the handler, lifetime by the scope: a completion of a subject with a handler is retained in the scope occurrence it completed in, and the record lives as long as that scope occurrence ([experiment result][cre-result], [`Compensation.completed`](../../../Kernel/Feature/Compensation.lean)). Retention is hidden semantic state, never reconstructed from publication ([experiment result][cre-result], [experiment scope][cre-scope]).
- A record carries the subject, its handler, the completed occurrence's identity and a completion ordinal; ordinals and identities are monotonic and never reused ([CBR contract][cbr-contract]). Active, interrupted or failed work completes nothing and retains nothing; an interrupting boundary path retains nothing ([CBR scope][cbr-scope], `TXC-RETAIN-01` in [TXC rules][txc-rules]).
- A boundary handler restores no context. A Compensation Event Sub-Process restores its parent's own variables as they were when the parent completed, never Task I/O, other Activities' local scopes or an entry-time copy, and reads every other name live through its context's chain (§13.5.4, §13.5.5, §10.3.5, §10.4.1; D9 of [Interpretation M9](#interpretation-m9), which supersedes the [CESP account][cesp-normative]'s copy of every visible variable). Records and snapshots are keyed by the complete runtime occurrence, so a later per-instance account for loops and Multi-Instance parents needs no new identity ([CESP account][cesp-normative]).
- When a Sub-Process completes, the records retained inside it stay compensable: a Sub-Process with a handler keeps them as children its handler's own throw compensates, and one without a handler passes them to its parent's scope occurrence (§13.5.5; `Compensable.children`), or, under `implicitCompensation = everySubProcess` (CIB-INT-0036, the cibseven profiles), is retained there as one group record whose children they are (I5). The old accounts excluded implicit and recursive compensation and left OMG BPMN21-403 unresolved ([CBR account][cbr-normative], [CTH scope][cth-scope]); admission refuses `compensation.implicit` when a handler-less Scope's records would reach a parent occurrence a Compensation throw shares, outside `everySubProcess`. A handler's own Compensation throw compensates the records its Scope kept, and the records no throw selects are dropped with the handler's context (§10.7.1, §13.5.5; I7).
- Leaving a scope occurrence without completing it drops every record retained in it ("presumed abort"; `State.leaveScope`), and ending the instance drops every record. The snapshot account retained a promoted root snapshot in the terminal state for a later parent host ([CESP scope][cesp-scope]); that retention is track work of PC-COMPENSATION-05, listed under the row below.

### Trigger and order

- A Compensation throw compensates the records retained in its own scope occurrence (BPMN 2.0.2 §10.7.2, §13.5.5): the run takes them out of retention atomically, so no other trigger can compensate them again, and the thrower continues only after every handler has completed; an empty set continues at once ([CTH basis][cth-normative], [CTH contract][cth-contract], [`Compensation.compensate`](../../../Kernel/Feature/Compensation.lean)). A throw with an `activityRef` compensates only that Activity's records; admission requires the Activity to be visible from the throw, at its own level or, from a Compensation Event Sub-Process, at its parent's level (§10.7.2, Tables 10.88, 10.89 and 10.94; I3). Only the synchronous form is admitted.
- Order is the reverse of dependency: a direct Sequence Flow from subject A, or from a boundary Event attached to A, to subject B, a name B reads that A can write, and two records of one subject each make the later handler complete before the earlier one's starts (I1); handlers without such a dependency run concurrently. Every pending handler none of whose successors is pending or running starts at once, in program order (§13.5.5; [CTH basis][cth-normative], [CSL account][csl-normative]). Completion chronology is not that order, and CIB's reverse creation-time order cannot implement it in general (`ProfileOptions.compensationOrder = dependency`). Gateway-mediated dependencies are not inferred ([CTH scope][cth-scope]).
- Each handler begins in a context scope occurrence of its own inside the run's scope, holding the restored context; its subject's child records move into that context, where a throw inside the handler compensates them ([`Compensation.beginHandler`](../../../Kernel/Feature/Compensation.lean)). The handler begins there as a token would: its input mappings read that context, it may do any work the kernel admits, and a Service Task handler's effect stays asynchronous, where CIB runs it inside the triggering command (CIB-LIM-0042); an unavailable Data Input has no flow to be held on and is the dynamic-only `compensation.handlerInputUnavailable`.
- A second trigger while a run is in progress in the same scope occurrence has no selected relation to the first and is refused (`compensation-concurrent-trigger`) ([CTH contract][cth-contract]).
- Compensation Start `isInterrupting`, Compensation Boundary `cancelActivity`, catch-side `waitForCompletion` and Association direction neither classify nor lower (Tables 10.87, 10.91, 10.92, 8.20; [CSL account][csl-normative]).

### Handler failure

- An Error a handler's effect reports, or an Error End Event inside a handler Sub-Process throws, and nothing inside the handler catches fails the process instance: the handler and its run fail, every token, item, occurrence and handler is removed, nothing continues, and the failed state publishes the run's trigger, the handler, the raising effect or End Event, the code and the message (`COMPH-FAIL-01`; [CTH contract][cth-contract], [`Compensation.failCompensation`](../../../Kernel/Feature/Compensation.lean)). BPMN 2.0.2 §13.3.2 names the handler states but not this disposition; it is a project interpretation, not BPMN or CIB transcription ([CTH basis][cth-normative]), selected by `ProfileOptions.compensationHandlerFailure = failsInstance`. A failed instance accepts no further input and is not a host failure; transport retries are never typed BPMN errors ([TXC rules][txc-rules]).
- Under `failsInstance` propagation never searches past a handler's context, so an event a handler leaves uncaught reaches the handler failure rule; under `propagatesFromTrigger` (CIB-INT-0038, the cibseven profiles) the search goes on from the handler context's parent, the scope occurrence the trigger ran in (I11; see [propagation](PROPAGATION-SEMANTICS-SPEC.md)).

### Transaction cancellation

- A Transaction is a Sub-Process whose cancellation interrupts its running work and compensates its completed eligible work; its Cancel Boundary Event takes control after rollback and compensation finish (BPMN 2.0.2 §10.3.5). Ordinary interruption is no implicit compensation trigger, and quiescence alone does not implement protocol success ([TXC basis][txc-normative]).
- A Cancel End Event raises Cancel from the scope occurrence it ran in. A caught Cancel clears the Transaction's interior, compensates the records retained in it, and only once the last handler has completed cancels the Transaction occurrence and takes the Cancel boundary's flows in the parent; the Transaction's normal outflow never fires (`TXC-CANCEL-01`, `TXC-EMPTY-01`, `TXC-JOIN-01`; [`Propagation`](../../../Kernel/Feature/Propagation.lean)). With no eligible record the boundary is taken in the same step; unfinished work disappears uncompensated. Records of nested scopes the cancellation interrupts are dropped with them.
- A handler failure during cancellation fails the instance as above, and neither the boundary nor the normal flow continues (`TXC-FAIL-01`, a project extension of `COMPH-FAIL-01`, not a hazard boundary). Regional cancellation preserves everything outside the Transaction's region except its continuation (`TXC-FRAME-01`).
- `method` is omitted or exactly `##Compensate`, the XSD default; other spellings are neither case-folded nor aliased (OMG BPMN2-60; [TXC basis][txc-normative]). Under `transactionMethods = compatibilityAsCompensate` (CIB-INT-0039, the cibseven profiles) `##Store` and `##Image` run as `##Compensate`; a protocol URI is refused under every profile (I14).
- Publication: the Transaction stays open while it compensates, interrupted work closes as cancelled, the handler's lifecycle is published, and the Transaction then closes as cancelled before the boundary occurrence; retention and trigger ownership are no public fields, and there is no public cancel command ([TXC public][txc-public]).
- The old account rejected a Cancel boundary on an ordinary Sub-Process, a non-interrupting Cancel boundary and a Cancel End outside a Transaction before lowering (§10.5.5; [TXC contract][txc-contract]). The kernel refuses the same set (§10.5.5): `boundary.cancel.host` for a Cancel boundary on anything but a Transaction, `boundary.cancel.nonInterrupting` for a non-interrupting one, and `throw.cancel.outsideTransaction` for a Cancel throw whose direct parent is not a Transaction (`Admission.Subscriptions.boundary`, `Admission.Propagation.throwRecord`) (PC-TX-02). It also refuses a Cancel End whose Transaction has no Cancel boundary (`throw.cancel.uncaught`: the boundary directs the flow after the cancellation, §10.3.5) and a second Cancel boundary on one Transaction (`boundary.cancel.duplicate`) (I13, [CIB-LIM-0043](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-lim-0043--cib-deploys-cancel-forms-bpmn-does-not-define-and-fails-or-strands-them)); a Cancel therefore always finds its Transaction's boundary, and `Propagation.exhausted` treats one that does not as the invariant failure `cancel-uncaught`.

#### I1 — Invocation order and completion chronology (PC-COMPENSATION-01)

- BPMN: §13.5.5 Operational Semantics: default compensation runs Compensation Activities "in reverse order of the execution of the original Activities, allowing for concurrency when there was no dependency", and it MUST consider these dependencies: a Sequence Flow between A and B "results in compensation of B to be performed before compensation of A"; "a data dependency between Activities A and B, e.g., through an IORules specification in B referring to data produced by A", likewise; two Activities of an Ad-Hoc Sub-Process, B before A if A completed before B started; instances of a loop or sequential Multi-Instance "in reverse order of their forward completion", parallel instances "can be compensated in parallel"; "If a Sub-Process A has a boundary Event connected to Activity B, then compensation of B MUST be performed before compensation of A if that particular Event occurred. This also applies to multi-instances and loops."
- CIB: `bpmn/helper/CompensationUtil.java:55-88` creates one concurrent compensating execution per retained subscription and signals every one at once, in reverse `created` order (`:78-87`); a waiting handler (a Human Task) is therefore open together with every other handler of the trigger, and only the start order of handlers that run synchronously follows creation time. The subscriptions reach that sort through a `HashSet` (`:191-205`), whose iteration follows `EventSubscriptionEntity.hashCode` over the identifier (`persistence/entity/EventSubscriptionEntity.java:331-336`), and `Collections.sort` is stable, so under a frozen clock equal `created` times keep the hash iteration order (the runner pins identifiers, `CibSevenEngineBundleFactory.ScenarioIds`).
- Witnesses: retained `compensation-order-chronology/*` (5): after the throw CIB opens `UserTask_UndoA`, `UserTask_UndoB` and `UserTask_UndoC` together, where the kernel holds `UserTask_UndoA` until `UserTask_UndoB` completes (B follows A by Sequence Flow); `compensation-multi-instance-bodies/*-sequential-in-turn` and `compensation-order-and-multiplicity/call-sequential-three-reviewers`: CIB opens the handlers of all instances together. `later-handler-first`, `with-message-before-a-completes` and `without-message` agree. Once the refusals of I3, I9 and I10 lift, `compensation-targeting/chain-handlers-forward`, `chain-handlers-reversed`, `end-untargeted` and `compensation-eligibility/*` keep disagreeing by this order.
- Relation: the kernel keeps `compensationOrder = dependency` under every profile (D1). B's handler completes before A's starts when (a) A's record completed before B's and a Sequence Flow leads directly from A to B, so a B reached by another path, or an earlier B of a loop, gives no order; (b) a Sequence Flow leads directly from a boundary Event attached to A to B: B's record exists only if B completed, so when every incoming flow of B leaves a boundary of A, the Event occurred, and admission refuses a B with another incoming flow (`compensation.dependency.boundary`), since its record cannot show which arrival it stems from; this order holds whichever record completed first, since a non-interrupting boundary's B can complete while A still runs; (c) a data dependency, which the kernel sees as a sound over-approximation: B completed after A (`Compensable.completion`) and an expression of B's element (its envelope and, for a Scope, its interior) reads a name A's element (with, for a Scope, its interior) can write: a name its output mappings, a Call's outputs, a caught subscription's outputs or a Multi-Instance element's output collection and data item target, or every name when A writes names chosen at run time (a User Task under `submission = installsVariables`); a task kind the compensation feature cannot inspect (none of User, Service, Call or Receive work) counts as reading and writing every name, which BPMN permits, since default compensation runs in reverse execution order and allows concurrency only without a dependency; an IORule pairs an Activity's own InputSet and OutputSet and names no other Activity, so it adds nothing beyond the reads; (d) repeated records of one subject run in reverse completion order, the instances of a parallel Multi-Instance Sub-Process included: §13.5.5 says they "can be compensated in parallel", which permits concurrency there but does not require it, and telling overlapping records from sequential ones needs a begin issue on `Compensable` ([#98](https://github.com/mbackschat/bpmn-lean-experiment/issues/98)). Ad-Hoc Sub-Processes are outside the IL. Gateway-mediated flows stay uninferred, as adopted above. CIB's order is a candidate deviation. Recorded as [CIB-DEV-0011](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-dev-0011--cib-starts-every-compensation-handler-of-one-trigger-at-once).
- Shape: `CompensationOrder.reverseCreation` names an order CIB does not have for waiting handlers; it becomes `allAtOnce` (CIB's), refused as `profile.compensationOrder.allAtOnce`. New admission code `compensation.dependency.boundary`. The data and same-subject rules read `Compensable.completion` and the program; no State change.

#### I2 — Which records an untargeted throw sees (PC-COMPENSATION-02, PC-COMPENSATION-05)

- BPMN: §10.7.2 and §13.5.5 Compensation Triggering: "If the Activity is clear from the context, it doesn't have to be specified and defaults to the current Activity. A typical scenario for that is an inline error handler of a SubProcess that cannot recover the error, and as a result would trigger compensation for that Sub-Process"; in a "global" context every completed Activity of the Process. Tables 10.88 and 10.89 compensate, with no Activity identified, "all successfully completed Activities visible from" the throw, where "Visible means" the throw "is contained in normal flow and at the same level of Sub-Process as the Activities" or "in a Compensation Event Sub-Process which is contained in the Sub-Process containing the Activities".
- CIB: `CompensationUtil.collectCompensateEventSubscriptionsForScope` (`CompensationUtil.java:184-206`) walks the flow scopes outward from the throw with `walkUntil` (`tree/ReferenceWalker.java:88-101`), whose post-visitor collects the subscriptions of every scope it moves to, up to and including the first scope that consumes compensation; `bpmn/parser/BpmnParse.java:3885` marks every Event Sub-Process as not consuming. A throw inside an Event Sub-Process therefore takes the union of the Event Sub-Process's own records and those of the Sub-Process holding it (source and retained); a throw inside a Compensation Event Sub-Process reaches the event-scope execution that holds the parent's inner subscriptions (I7).
- Witnesses: retained `compensation-event-subprocess-throws/error-esp-throws-untargeted`: the untargeted throw in an Error Event Sub-Process opens `UserTask_UndoTask` of its parent's completed Task; before I2 the kernel compensated nothing (its run scope was the Event Sub-Process occurrence) and continued to *Continue after the recovery*.
- Relation: the kernel follows CIB's union (D15), which is BPMN-consistent: the Activities at the same level as an Event Sub-Process's throw and those of the Sub-Process its inline handler compensates. An untargeted throw takes the records retained in every scope occurrence from the throw's own outward through Event Sub-Process occurrences, up to and including the first scope occurrence of another kind. A compensation-handler context (`ScopeRole.compensationHandler`) ends the walk and stands for the Compensation Event Sub-Process's parent, since `Compensation.beginHandler` moved that parent's records into it; without the stop, a throw inside a Compensation Event Sub-Process would reach the outer run's scope and fail as `compensation-concurrent-trigger`. A targeted throw resolves its Activity among the same scope occurrences (I3). The run's scope is the throw's own scope occurrence, where its handlers begin; `compensation-concurrent-trigger` refuses a trigger while a run is in progress in any scope occurrence the walk covers. No register entry: the retained answer becomes agreeing evidence.
- Shape: none; the walk reads `ScopeKind.eventSubProcess` and `Ctx.scopeRole` on the throw's scope chain.

#### I3 — Targeted throws and Compensation End Events (PC-COMPENSATION-02)

- BPMN: §10.7.2 ("The Activity that needs to be compensated is referenced"), Table 10.94 (`activityRef: Activity [0..1]`), §13.5.6 Compensation End Events, and Tables 10.88 and 10.89: "The Activity MUST be visible from" the throw, i.e. the throw "is contained in normal flow at the same level of Sub-Process" or "in a Compensation Event Sub-Process which is contained in the Sub-Process containing the Activity".
- CIB: `BpmnParse.java:1695-1720` requires `activityRef` to name an activity at the throw's Sub-Process level and, from any Event Sub-Process (`triggeredByEvent`, not only a Compensation one), at its parent's level, refusing any other at deployment; `CompensationUtil.java:215-226` selects the subscriptions of that activity, keyed by `getSubscriptionActivityId` (`:239-254`: a Multi-Instance subject's body, else the subject's handler, else the Sub-Process itself); a reference to an element that retains nothing, such as a Start Event, selects nothing and the throw continues at once (`bpmn/behavior/CompensationEventActivityBehavior.java:45-54`). A consumed subscription is deleted when its handler starts (`event/CompensationEventHandler.java:45`).
- Witnesses: retained `compensation-targeting/*` (6), `compensation-regions/inner-throw-then-region-targeted`, `region-targeted-after-completion`, `compensation-event-subprocess-throws/error-esp-throws-targeted` (all committed in CIB; the kernel refuses `throw.compensation.activity`); `compensation-activity-ref-refusals/inner-throw-names-outer-activity` and `outer-throw-names-inner-activity` (CIB refuses at deployment, as the kernel does); `throw-names-a-start-event` (CIB commits with an empty set).
- Relation: the kernel admits `activityRef` naming an Activity visible from the throw (its own level, or from a Compensation Event Sub-Process its parent's level) and compensates that subject's records in I2's walk, in I1's order; a reference to any other element is refused at admission (`throw.compensation.activityLevel`, `throw.compensation.notActivity`). A reference from another Event Sub-Process (an Error, Escalation, Message, Signal, Timer or Conditional one) to its parent's level violates the visibility MUST, which CIB accepts (retained `error-esp-throws-targeted`): an extension, refused as `throw.compensation.activityLevel` under `compensationReferenceScope = visible` and admitted under `eventSubProcessParent`. Recorded as [CIB-EXT-0046](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-ext-0046--a-compensation-throw-whose-activityref-names-no-activity-compensates-nothing) (D2 for the Start Event case) and [CIB-EXT-0047](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-ext-0047--cib-resolves-a-targeted-compensation-throw-from-any-event-sub-process-at-its-parents-level).
- Shape: `ProfileOptions.compensationReferenceScope : CompensationReferenceScope` with `visible` (default) and `eventSubProcessParent`; `CompensationThrow.activity` exists.

#### I5 — Records of a completed Sub-Process without a handler, and Transaction success (PC-COMPENSATION-03, PC-TX-01)

- BPMN: §10.5.1: "The compensation handler is either user defined or implicit. The implicit compensation handler of a Sub Process calls all compensation handlers of its enclosed Activities in the reverse order of Sequence Flow dependencies." Tables 10.88 and 10.89: "To be compensated, an Activity MUST have a boundary Compensation Event, or contain a Compensation Event Sub-Process." §10.7.1 and §13.5.5 tie default compensation of a Sub-Process to an attribute `compensable` that neither the CMOF nor the XSD defines (OMG BPMN21-403). The standard is inconsistent: §10.5.1 makes every Sub-Process compensable through its implicit handler, the tables only those with a handler. Transaction (Table 10.21 and its outcomes): successful completion leaves by normal Sequence Flow; ordinary interruption causes no compensation.
- CIB: `bpmn/behavior/SubProcessActivityBehavior.java:61-66` calls `CompensationUtil.createEventScopeExecution` (`CompensationUtil.java:100-152`) on every completed Sub-Process and Transaction (`BpmnParse.java:3902-3911` gives a Transaction the Sub-Process behavior), and `MultiInstanceActivityBehavior.java:172-175` on every completed Multi-Instance body; when the scope holds compensate subscriptions or a Compensation Event Sub-Process (`CompensationUtil.java:108`), it keeps the inner subscriptions under an event-scope execution, copies the Sub-Process's local variables into it by value (`:118-124`) and subscribes the Sub-Process itself, without a handler, to its parent (`:143-151`, `:167-178`); compensating that subscription descends into the event scope and compensates the inner records as one group (`CompensationEventHandler.java:61-64`). Every Sub-Process with compensable content is thus implicitly compensable.
- Witnesses: retained, committed in CIB, refused by the kernel as `compensation.implicit`: `compensation-regions/*` (4), `transaction-paths/success-then-outer-throw`, `cancel-end`, `error-end`, `compensation-multi-instance-bodies/regions-*` (2), `compensation-context-live-outer/default-handler-reads-and-writes-outer`, `compensation-event-subprocess-throws/error-esp-completes-then-outer-throw`, `transaction-and-error-exits/error-end-in-subprocess`; `compensation-context/*` has no CIB answer (CIB-LIM-0048). Agreeing: `transaction-and-error-exits/transaction-completes-while-boundary-path-runs`.
- Relation: CIB's implicit compensation of every Sub-Process with compensable content is its interpretation of the inconsistency between §10.5.1 and Tables 10.88 and 10.89, which BPMN21-403 leaves open (D3). Recorded as [CIB-INT-0036](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-int-0036--cib-compensates-every-completed-sub-process-without-a-handler-as-the-group-of-its-inner-records).
- Kernel meaning: under `refused` `Compensation.completed` moves a handler-less Scope's records to its parent occurrence, flattening the group; under `everySubProcess` the completed Scope with at least one inner record is retained as one record without a handler whose `children` are its inner records, so a throw naming the Sub-Process selects the group (I3), the group is one subject for I1's order relative to its siblings, and compensating it runs its children as a nested run in a context occurrence holding the Sub-Process's snapshot (I12). A Scope with no inner record retains nothing, as in CIB. The nested run ends with its own `Resume.group` (shape S1), which leaves the group's context and advances the outer run, as a completed handler does.
- Shape: `Compensable.handler : Option ElementId` (`none` = a group); `Resume.group` (S1); `ProfileOptions.implicitCompensation : ImplicitCompensation` with `refused` (default, standards, refusal `compensation.implicit`) and `everySubProcess` (cibseven).

#### I6 — An Error leaving a Sub-Process with completed compensable work (PC-COMPENSATION-03, PC-TX-01)

- BPMN: inconsistent. §10.7.3 and §13.5.5 Relationship between Error Handling and Compensation: "If no error Event Sub-Process is specified for a particular Sub-Process and a particular error, the default behavior is to automatically call compensation for all contained Activities of that Sub-Process". Against it, §10.3.5 Transaction outcome 2: "other mechanisms for interrupting a Transaction Sub-Process will not cause compensation (e.g., Error, Timer, and anything for a non-Transaction Activity)", and the presumed-abort principle of the same clauses: "Compensation of a failed Activity results in a null operation", and the error handler is responsible "to ensure that no further compensation will be necessary". Transaction outcome 3: a Hazard "is interrupted (without compensation)".
- CIB: an Error leaving a Sub-Process interrupts it without `doLeave`, so no event-scope execution is created and its subscriptions are deleted with it (`SubProcessActivityBehavior.java:61-66` runs only on completion): nothing is compensated (source and retained).
- Witnesses: retained `transaction-and-error-exits/error-end-in-subprocess` (CIB never opens `UserTask_UndoWork`), `transaction-paths/error-end`, `error-end-in-transaction` (agree).
- Relation: CIB reads §10.3.5 and presumed abort over §10.7.3's default, an interpretation; the kernel reads the same under every profile (D4). `compensatesContained` would also leave open whether the catching boundary or Event Sub-Process runs before or after the default compensation. Recorded as [CIB-INT-0037](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-int-0037--cib-compensates-nothing-inside-a-sub-process-an-error-leaves).
- Shape: `ProfileOptions.errorExitCompensation : ErrorExitCompensation` with `drops` (default, selected by every profile) and `compensatesContained`, a refused variant: `Admission.Profile.options` refuses it as `profile.errorExitCompensation.compensatesContained` only when the program has a Sub-Process that retains compensable work and that an Error can leave (a program predicate passed like `abnormalScope`), never every program of a profile.

#### I7 — Recursive compensation, Compensation Event Sub-Processes, consumption and rethrow (PC-COMPENSATION-05)

- BPMN: §10.7.1 and §13.5.5: a Compensation Event Sub-Process "can recursively trigger compensation for Activities contained in its parent" and restores the parent's snapshot; consuming the trigger excludes implicit deeper handlers unless rethrown (PC-COMPENSATION-05's own acceptance).
- CIB: the Compensation Event Sub-Process runs in the completed Sub-Process's event-scope execution, which holds the inner subscriptions (`CompensationEventHandler.java:70-71`, `CompensationUtil.java:100-152`); its throw collects them by I2's walk; the trigger joins only non-event-scope executions, so inner records nobody rethrows stay uncompensated (`CompensationEventActivityBehavior.java:65-76`).
- Witnesses: refused by the kernel as `compensation.recursive`: `compensation-event-subprocess-multi-instance/*` (4, CIB opens one parent handler per instance and then fails the command that completes one, CIB-LIM-0045), `compensation-event-subprocess-nesting/*`, `compensation-event-subprocess-throws/throws-parent-activity` and `compensation-handler-consumption/esp-consumes-one-of-two` (no CIB answer: the state observation fails inside CIB, see the probe results), `throws-nested-activity` (CIB refuses at deployment: the reference is not at the Event Sub-Process's parent level, I3).

#### I8 — A boundary handler on a Sub-Process (PC-COMPENSATION-05)

#### I9 — Multi-Instance, Call and effect subjects (PC-COMPENSATION-03)

- BPMN: §10.7.2 and §13.5.5 Compensation Triggering: "Triggering compensation for the Multi-Instance Sub-Process individually triggers compensation for all instances within the current scope. If compensation is specified via a boundary compensation handler, this boundary compensation handler also is invoked once for each instance of the Multi-Instance Sub-Process in the current scope." §13.5.5 Operational Semantics: "In case the Activity is a multi-instance or loop, the Compensation Activity is triggered only once, too, and thus has to compensate the effects of all instances." The per-instance sentences name only a Multi-Instance Sub-Process, so for a Multi-Instance Task or Call only "triggered only once" applies.
- CIB: every completed instance leaves its own subscription at the Multi-Instance body (`AbstractBpmnActivityBehavior.java:69-74`), for Tasks, Call Activities and Sub-Processes alike; instances a completion condition cancelled leave none; zero instances leave none (retained). - Witnesses: retained, refused by the kernel as `activity.compensationHandler` or `activity.multiInstanceBody`: `compensation-order-and-multiplicity/user-handler-two-items` (two handlers), `user-handler-zero-items` (none), `user-parallel-quorum-ends-after-one` (one, for the completed instance), `call-parallel-three-reviewers`, `call-sequential-three-reviewers` (three), `compensation-multi-instance-bodies/tasks-*` (three), `compensation-effect-and-call-records/call-completed-then-throw` (a Call subject). Agreeing: `success-then-throw`, `business-failure-ineligible`, `compensation-eligibility/completed-then-throw`, `interrupted-then-throw`.

When the body completes, `Compensation.completed` retains one record without a handler for the Multi-Instance element in the body's scope occurrence, with the instance records as `children`, as CIB's event scope does, and the body adds no second record with the handler. Its nested run (S1) runs the children in reverse completion order, for a parallel Multi-Instance too (I1). A Multi-Instance Task or Call retains nothing per instance: the body's completion retains one record with the handler, which runs once, also after zero instances. A Call subject is retained like a Task; the called instance's own records end with it.

- Shape: `ProfileOptions.multiInstanceCompensation : MultiInstanceCompensation` with `perSubProcessInstance` (default, every profile) and `perInstance` (CIB-DEV-0012), refused as `profile.multiInstanceCompensation.perInstance` only when the program has a Multi-Instance Task or Call with a compensation handler; `Resume.group` (S1). Admission lifts `activity.compensationHandler` for Call Activities and Multi-Instance subjects and `activity.multiInstanceBody` where only the subject's handler blocks it.

#### I11 — An Error, Escalation or Terminate leaving a handler (PC-COMPENSATION-01, PC-COMPENSATION-04)

- BPMN: §13.3.2 names the handler states but no disposition for a failing handler; §10.5.1: "Error triggers are critical and suspend execution at the location of throwing. Escalations are non critical and execution continues at the location of throwing"; §13.5.6 Terminate ends its own scope.
- CIB: a handler's compensating execution is a child of the throwing execution (`CompensationUtil.java:62-75`), so an Error or Escalation the handler raises propagates from there to catchers enclosing the throw; an Error with no catcher ends the throwing path (CIB-INT-0013, `unhandledError = endsThrowingPath`), so the handler completes and the trigger continues (retained).
- Witnesses: retained `compensation-handler-failure-at-wait/error-caught-handler-fails-after-wait` and `escalation-caught-*` (the Error boundary on `SubProcess_Outer` catches it; `UserTask_Caught` runs), `error-uncaught-*` and `escalation-uncaught-*` (the run continues to `UserTask_After`); the kernel answers `compensation-handler-error-without-effect` or `compensation-handler-escalation` today. `compensation-handler-terminate/*`: CIB fails the command that starts the handler Sub-Process at the handler's fork, before any Terminate (CIB-LIM-0047), so CIB gives no answer on a Terminate inside a handler; the kernel's disposition is the profile reading below.
- Relation (D8): under the cibseven profiles (`propagatesFromTrigger`) a search that reaches a handler context (`ScopeRole.compensationHandler`) continues from that context's parent scope occurrence, the run's scope where the throw ran, not from `CompensationRun.trigger`, an `OccurrenceRef` that names no scope occurrence; an uncaught Error follows the profile's `unhandledError`. A handler that ends without completing leaves through `Step.complete`'s `.raised` arm, which runs no continuation, so `Hooks.handlerCompleted` never fires and the run would wait forever; that arm therefore calls a new `Hooks.handlerEnded` for continuation `.compensation run`, with which `Kernel.Feature.Compensation` leaves the handler context and advances the run as `handlerCompleted` does, if the run still exists. A catcher that interrupts a scope occurrence holding the run drops the run when the catch is decided (`Compensation.dropWithin` through `PropagationHooks.dropRuns`), before the leave the raising handler queued reaches `handlerEnded`, so a dropped run begins no further handler, and the trigger continues only when no catcher interrupted it; a begin into a scope occurrence that is gone is the invariant failure `begin-into-missing-scope` (`Step.requireScope`); under `failsInstance` `handlerEnded` changes nothing, since the failure that follows ends the instance. Under the standards profile `failsInstance` stays (COMPH-FAIL-01) and is extended to an Error End Event inside a handler, whose End Event occurrence the failure publishes as its origin (`InstanceFailure` needs no raised kind, S9); an Escalation is not widened, since §10.5.1 continues execution at its thrower, and stays refused as `compensation-handler-escalation`. A Terminate inside a handler Sub-Process: the standards profile refuses the program as `profile.abnormalScopeEnd.withoutOutflow` (`AdmissionRegistry.abnormalScope` covers a Terminate in any Sub-Process); under the cibseven profiles (`abnormalScopeEnd = completesNormally`) it ends the handler Sub-Process, which completes normally, so `handlerCompleted` fires. CIB gives no answer there (CIB-LIM-0047). Recorded as [CIB-INT-0038](../../CIB-BPMN-RELATION-REGISTER.md#cib-int-0038--cib-propagates-a-compensation-handlers-error-or-escalation-from-the-compensation-trigger).
- Shape: `CompensationHandlerFailure.propagatesFromTrigger` (new variant). The search reads the handler context's parent scope occurrence, so State needs no field. `Hooks.handlerEnded` is declared by the shape commit; its implementation and its call in `Step.complete` land with this item's behavior commit. `compensation-handler-error-without-effect` ends under both profiles; `compensation-handler-escalation` stays under the standards profile; `compensation-handler-cancel` becomes an invariant failure (R10, I13).

#### I15 — Multi-Instance and concurrently cancelled Transactions (PC-TX-03)

- BPMN: §10.3.5 Transaction with Multi-Instance characteristics is not excluded; the Cancel boundary belongs to the Activity.

- Relation: agreement with BPMN's interruption rule (records of a cancelled Activity's completed bodies are dropped with it; ordinary interruption is no implicit compensation). A Cancel from a body reaches the Multi-Instance body occurrence, since instance roles carry no boundaries (`Step.scopeRole`); `Propagation.cancelledScope` makes the run's scope the cancelling body's Transaction occurrence, the nearest of the Transaction on the chain from the raise, so `clearInside` clears only that occurrence's interior, the run compensates the records retained there, `leave` is the Multi-Instance body occurrence and `resume` occurs the Cancel boundary (`Signal.compensate` carries the scope and `leave` apart). #### I18 — The boundary Events of a Transaction its Cancel rolls back (PC-TX-02, PC-TX-03)

- BPMN: §10.3.5 Transaction outcome 2: the Cancel boundary "will direct the flow after the Transaction has been rolled back and all compensation has been completed"; §13.3.4: a Cancel End Event abnormally terminates the Sub-Process and aborts its Transaction; §13.3.2 (Figure 13.2): an interrupting Event takes the Activity from Active to Terminating, which has no "Activity Interrupted" edge, and attached non-interrupting Event Handlers "MAY be created as long as the main Activity is in state Active".
- CIB: the Cancel End starts with `INTERRUPT_FLOW_SCOPE` (`BpmnParse.java:3163-3168`), whose `TRANSITION_INTERRUPT_FLOW_SCOPE` (`AbstractPvmAtomicOperationTransitionNotifyListenerTake.java:43-45`) interrupts the Transaction's scope execution (`PvmAtomicOperationInterruptScope.java:42`); `ExecutionEntity.interrupt` deletes its jobs and its event subscriptions other than compensation ones (`ExecutionEntity.java:575-588`, `:1064-1072`). A boundary's Timer job and its Message, Signal and Conditional subscriptions live on the attached Activity's execution (`BpmnParse.java:3290-3300`, `:3424`, `:3439`, `:3455`, `:3503`); a Multi-Instance Activity's live on its body (`:3291-3296`), which one instance's Cancel does not interrupt.
- Witnesses: retained `transaction-cancel-mixed/cancel-with-effect-unanswered` (the Timer is gone after the Cancel); probes `transaction-cancel-mixed/deadline-passes-while-cancel-compensates` (CIB rejects the Timer fire during the run) and `transaction-multi-instance/parallel-cancel-deadline-during-run` (the body's Timer fires during one instance's run and cancels every instance with the run); all three agree. `refund-business-failure` and `refund-incident-retried` lose `Boundary_Deadline` after the Cancel and keep their owners; `subjects-effect-first` and `subjects-user-first` change with no CIB answer.
- Relation: agreement, no register entry. The reading covers event-triggered boundaries only (Timer, Message, Signal, Conditional; Timer witnessed, the others by source reading): when a Cancel rolls a Transaction back as a whole, `Propagation.leaveOccurrence` disarms the items attached to its Activity occurrence (`State.disarm`). Catchers of raised events are no items and stay reachable during the run: under the cibseven profiles a handler's Error or Escalation propagates from the trigger (CIB-INT-0038), CIB-LIM-0046 for a synchronous handler's failure; under the standards profile COMPH-FAIL-01 ends the search at the handler (Figure 13.2: Terminating to Failed). For a Multi-Instance Transaction BPMN does not settle when one instance's Cancel affects the Activity; D12 governs: the Cancel boundary takes effect once that instance's rollback completes (§10.3.5), until then only the instance's Transaction is Terminating, so the body's boundaries stay armed.
- Defect class: interrupting a scope occurrence left the attached items of its Activity armed. Its sibling, an interrupting Event Sub-Process whose parent's boundaries stay armed until the parent completes ([scopes semantics](SCOPES-SEMANTICS-SPEC.md#event-sub-processes), `Kernel/Feature/Scopes.lean:62-65`), stays outside I18, tracked with its CIB probe plan and the Conditional hypothesis below in [issue #100](https://github.com/mbackschat/bpmn-lean-experiment/issues/100); the adopted Scopes rule does not change in M9.
- Hypothesis, untested, no code: a write in the step that reaches the Cancel End is offered before the Cancel is caught, so a Conditional boundary of the Transaction it satisfies fires first (interrupting: no Cancel runs; non-interrupting: its path and the Cancel's run both start; kernel scratch run, 2026-11-12), and only later writes find the boundary disarmed. CIB likely orders the same, as a submission's variable events reach Conditional subscriptions before the task leaves (CIB-DEV-0005); no witness separates it.
- Shape: none.

- D6 (I9): a Multi-Instance Sub-Process per instance, a Multi-Instance Task or Call once, under every profile; CIB-DEV-0012 recorded and not selected.

- D8 (I11): `propagatesFromTrigger` under the cibseven profiles; an Error End under `failsInstance` for the standards profile; no Escalation widening. `propagatesFromTrigger` rests on committed retained answers only, never on a rolled-back command (CIB-LIM-0046, CIB-LIM-0047).
